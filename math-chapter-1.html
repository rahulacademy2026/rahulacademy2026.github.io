End-of-Chapter Exercises 1–16

Q1. Where do the coordinate axes intersect?

Concept: The x-axis and y-axis cross at the origin.

Steps: At the intersection, both the horizontal coordinate x and vertical coordinate y are zero.

Final answer: Origin = (0, 0).

Exam tip: Remember that the origin is the starting point for both axes.

Q2. W has x-coordinate −5. What can we say about H on the line through W parallel to the y-axis?

Concept: A vertical line has the same x-coordinate at every point.

Step 1: Since W has x-coordinate −5, the line through W parallel to the y-axis is x = −5.

Step 2: Every point H on this line has the form H = (−5, y).

Final answer: H's x-coordinate is −5. If y > 0 it lies in Quadrant II; if y < 0 it lies in Quadrant III; if y = 0 it lies on the negative x-axis.

Q3. R(3, 0), A(0, −2), M(−5, −2), P(−5, 2)

Concept: Horizontal segments are parallel to the x-axis; vertical segments are parallel to the y-axis.

(i) Perpendicular sides

AM goes from (0, −2) to (−5, −2), so y stays fixed and AM is horizontal. MP goes from (−5, −2) to (−5, 2), so x stays fixed and MP is vertical.

Answer: AM ⟂ MP.

(ii) Side parallel to an axis

Answer: AM ∥ x-axis and MP ∥ y-axis.

(iii) Mirror-image points

M and P have the same x-coordinate and opposite y-coordinates: −2 and 2.

Answer: M(−5, −2) and P(−5, 2) are reflections across the x-axis.

Q4. Construct a right triangle using Z(5, −6)

Concept: Horizontal and vertical lines meet at a right angle.

One valid construction: Choose I = (5, 0) and N = (0, −6), with Z = (5, −6).

Step 1: IZ is vertical. Its length is |0 − (−6)| = 6 units.

Step 2: ZN is horizontal. Its length is |5 − 0| = 5 units.

Step 3: The two sides meet at Z at 90°. By Pythagoras, IN = √(6² + 5²) = √61 units.

Final answer: One possible right triangle has perpendicular sides IZ = 6 units and ZN = 5 units, and hypotenuse IN = √61 units. Other valid constructions may give different lengths.

Q5. What if the coordinate system had no negative numbers?

Concept: Negative coordinates are needed for points to the left of the y-axis or below the x-axis.

Without negative numbers, points with negative x- or y-coordinates could not be represented.

Final answer: No. We could not locate every point on the two-dimensional plane.

Q6. Are M(−3, −4), A(0, 0), and G(6, 8) collinear?

Concept: If slopes from the same point to two other points are equal, the three points lie on one straight line.

Step 1 — slope AM:

mAM = (−4 − 0)/(−3 − 0) = 4/3

Step 2 — slope AG:

mAG = (8 − 0)/(6 − 0) = 8/6 = 4/3

Step 3: The slopes are equal.

Final answer: Yes, M, A and G are collinear.

Exam tip: Show both slope calculations before writing the conclusion.

Q7. Are R(−5, −1), B(−2, −5), and C(4, −12) collinear?

Concept: Collinear points must have equal slopes between pairs of points.

Step 1 — slope RB:

mRB = [−5 − (−1)]/[−2 − (−5)] = −4/3

Step 2 — slope BC:

mBC = [−12 − (−5)]/[4 − (−2)] = −7/6

Step 3: Since −4/3 ≠ −7/6, the slopes are different.

Final answer: No, R, B and C are not collinear.

Q8(i). Make a right-angled isosceles triangle with the origin as one vertex.

Choose O = (0, 0), A = (3, 0), and B = (0, 3).

OA = 3 units and OB = 3 units. OA is horizontal and OB is vertical, so OA ⟂ OB.

Final answer: Triangle OAB is right-angled at O and is isosceles because OA = OB.

Q8(ii). Make an isosceles triangle with one vertex in Quadrant III and another in Quadrant IV.

Choose O = (0, 0), A = (−2, −2) in Quadrant III, and B = (2, −2) in Quadrant IV.

OA = √[(-2)² + (-2)²] = √8
OB = √[(2)² + (-2)²] = √8
Final answer: OA = OB, so triangle OAB is isosceles and meets the quadrant condition.

Q9. Is M the midpoint of ST?

Concept: The midpoint of S(x₁,y₁) and T(x₂,y₂) is ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2).

(i) S(−3, 0), M(0, 0), T(3, 0)

Midpoint = ((−3+3)/2, (0+0)/2) = (0, 0)
Answer: Yes, M is the midpoint.

(ii) S(2, 3), M(3, 4), T(4, 5)

Midpoint = ((2+4)/2, (3+5)/2) = (3, 4)
Answer: Yes, M is the midpoint.

(iii) S(0, 0), M(0, 5), T(0, −10)

Midpoint = ((0+0)/2, (0−10)/2) = (0, −5)
Answer: No. The midpoint is (0, −5), not (0, 5).

(iv) S(−8, 7), M(0, −2), T(6, −3)

Midpoint = ((−8+6)/2, (7−3)/2) = (−1, 2)
Answer: No. The midpoint is (−1, 2), not (0, −2).

Q10. M(−7, 1) is the midpoint of A(3, −4) and B(x, y). Find B.

Concept: Apply the midpoint formula to both coordinates.

Step 1 — x-coordinate:

(3 + x)/2 = −7
3 + x = −14
x = −17

Step 2 — y-coordinate:

(−4 + y)/2 = 1
−4 + y = 2
y = 6
Final answer: B = (−17, 6).

Q11. Find the trisection points of A(4, 7) and B(16, −2).

Concept: Trisection points divide a segment into three equal parts.

Step 1: Change from A to B is (16−4, −2−7) = (12, −9).

Step 2 — P, closer to A: Add one-third of the change to A: (4,7) + (4,−3) = (8,4).

Step 3 — Q, closer to B: Add two-thirds of the change to A: (4,7) + (8,−6) = (12,1).

Final answer: P = (8, 4), Q = (12, 1).

Q12. A(1, −8), B(−4, 7), C(−7, −4): circle centred at O(0, 0)

Concept: The distance from the origin to (x,y) is √(x²+y²).

Step 1: OA = √(1² + (−8)²) = √65.

Step 2: OB = √((−4)² + 7²) = √65.

Step 3: OC = √((−7)² + (−4)²) = √65.

Conclusion: A, B and C lie on the same circle centred at O with radius √65 units.

Classify D(−5, 6)

OD = √(25 + 36) = √61 < √65
Answer: D is inside the circle.

Classify E(0, 9)

OE = √(0² + 9²) = 9; and 9 > √65
Answer: E is outside the circle.

Q13. Triangle ABC has midpoints D(5, 1), E(6, 5), F(0, 3). Find A, B and C.

Concept: Assuming D, E and F are the midpoints of BC, CA and AB respectively, use the midpoint equations.

Step 1: Since D is midpoint of BC, E of CA, and F of AB:

A = E + F − D
B = F + D − E
C = D + E − F

Step 2 — Find A:

A = (6+0−5, 5+3−1) = (1, 7)

Step 3 — Find B:

B = (0+5−6, 3+1−5) = (−1, −1)

Step 4 — Find C:

C = (5+6−0, 1+5−3) = (11, 3)
Final answer: A = (1, 7), B = (−1, −1), C = (11, 3). This uses the usual convention D midpoint of BC, E midpoint of CA, F midpoint of AB.

Q14. City roads are 200 m apart; intersections are numbered (i, j).

Concept: An ordered pair identifies an intersection by its street numbers in the two directions.

If the first number refers to one street direction and the second number to the perpendicular direction, (4,3) and (3,4) identify different intersections because the order is reversed.

Answer: Under the usual ordered-pair convention, there are 2 distinct intersections: (4,3) and (3,4). Use the exact axis/numbering convention shown in the question's road diagram.

Q15. A screen is 800 × 600. Circle A(100,150), radius 80; circle B(250,230), radius 100.

(i) Does any part of either circle lie outside the screen?

Circle A: x ranges from 100−80 = 20 to 100+80 = 180; y ranges from 150−80 = 70 to 150+80 = 230. All are within 0–800 horizontally and 0–600 vertically.

Circle B: x ranges from 250−100 = 150 to 350; y ranges from 230−100 = 130 to 330. These are also within the screen.

Answer: No. Both circles lie entirely inside the screen.

(ii) Do the circles intersect?

Step 1: Distance between centres = √((250−100)² + (230−150)²) = √(22500+6400) = √28900 = 170.

Step 2: Sum of radii = 80+100 = 180; difference = 100−80 = 20.

Step 3: Since 20 < 170 < 180, the circles intersect at two points.

Final answer: Yes, the circles intersect at two points.

Q16. A(2, 1), B(−1, 2), C(−2, −1), D(1, −2): is ABCD a square?

Concept: A square has four equal sides and right angles. Equal diagonals also help verify it.

Step 1 — Side lengths:

AB² = (−1−2)² + (2−1)² = 9+1 = 10
BC² = (−2+1)² + (−1−2)² = 1+9 = 10
CD² = (1+2)² + (−2+1)² = 9+1 = 10
DA² = (2−1)² + (1+2)² = 1+9 = 10

All four sides have length √10.

Step 2 — Diagonals:

AC² = (−2−2)² + (−1−1)² = 16+4 = 20
BD² = (1+1)² + (−2−2)² = 4+16 = 20

Both diagonals have length √20. The diagonals are equal and the four sides are equal; adjacent side vectors also have dot product (−3)(−1) + (1)(−3) = 0, so adjacent sides are perpendicular.

Final answer: Yes, ABCD is a square. Side length = √10 units, so area = (√10)² = 10 square units.

Chapter recap

  • Coordinates are written in the order (x, y).
  • The origin is (0, 0).
  • A vertical line has constant x-coordinate; a horizontal line has constant y-coordinate.
  • Midpoint formula: ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2).
  • Distance from the origin to (x, y): √(x²+y²).
  • Compare slopes to test collinearity.
